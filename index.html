    About Me

    I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the AI in Genomics Lab, Department of Human Genetics, School of Medicine at Emory University. Since June 2026 I have been building agentic AI for transcriptomic and molecular profiles of Alzheimer's disease and schizophrenia, and developing machine learning methods for spatial multi-omics integration.

    I earned my Ph.D. in the School of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Georgia (UGA) in May 2026, advised by Prof. Tianming Liu. My dissertation, "A Video-LLM Agent for Personalized, Trustworthy, and Vision-Centric Biomedical Application," studied how video-language models can watch, remember, and reason over real clinical behavior. One result was AdCare-VLM (Journal of Biomedical Informatics), which monitors long-term medication adherence from patient videos. Alongside the Ph.D., I completed a master's-level Post-Bacc Diploma in International Law at UGA, and I hold a B.Sc. in Information and Communication Technology from Mawlana Bhashani Science and Technology University (MBSTU), Bangladesh.

    My journey. My path into AI has crossed several fields. As an undergraduate in the Bio-Photomatix group at MBSTU, I designed photonic crystal fiber and surface plasmon resonance biosensors for cancer-cell, hemoglobin, and fuel-adulteration detection. I then spent two years as a Software Engineer at Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh. I was promoted to Senior Software Engineer in 2021 and led the Wearable Notification team behind Galaxy Watches, Z-Fold phones, earbuds, and AR/VR glasses. At UGA I first joined the Photonics and Soft Robotics Lab (Prof. Mable P. Fok) and applied machine learning to microwave photonics, stealth RF systems, and bio-inspired soft water robots. I then moved to Prof. Tianming Liu's lab to work on video LLMs, multimodal memory, and medical AI agents. Today I bring that toolkit to genomics. Across these areas I have published 15+ papers in IEEE, Springer, Elsevier, Optica, and NeurIPS venues, with 800+ citations (h-index 12, Google Scholar).

    Research Interest: I am broadly interested in Agentic AI for Biomedicine, Multimodal Medical AI, Vision Large Language Models (VLLMs), Computational Genomics, and Trustworthy Machine Learning.

    Current focus (Emory):

    • Agentic AI for Genomics: LLM agents that reason over transcriptomic and molecular profiles to study Alzheimer's disease and schizophrenia
    • Spatial Multi-omics Integration: Machine learning to map the spatial molecular architecture of brain disorders at cohort scale
    • Memory-Augmented Medical Agents: Structured multimodal memory (e.g., MSM-Mem, NeurIPS 2026) for long-horizon, personalized clinical reasoning
    • Reinforcement and Meta-Learning: PPO, RLHF, and GRPO-based methods for self-corrective reasoning and adaptive biomedical agents
    • Trustworthy AI: Interpretability, robustness, and reliability of AI in safety-critical healthcare settings

    Previous research:

    • Video and Vision-Language Models (UGA): Video-image alignment for vision-centric tasks (NeurIPS 2025). I also fine-tuned Video-LLaVA (Vicuna-7B/13B) on podcast and psychiatric-interview videos, improving video understanding by 10%. Other work covered RAG and supervised fine-tuning for visual-spatial reasoning, plus memory-efficient training
    • ML for Photonics and Soft Robotics (UGA): GAN-based data augmentation for microwave frequency measurement, deep learning prediction of fiber optical properties, and ML-assisted locomotion tracking of soft water robots (RoboSoft 2024)
    • Optical Biosensing (MBSTU): Plasmonic and photonic crystal fiber sensors for cancer, hemoglobin, CFC/HCFC, and fuel-adulteration detection

    Looking ahead. My goal is to build AI agents that clinicians and biologists can trust: systems that combine molecular, imaging, and behavioral data, remember patient context over time, and explain how they reached a conclusion. In the near term, I aim to turn spatial multi-omics and agentic reasoning into tools that uncover the biology of neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disease. I also want them to be personalized, efficient, and accountable enough for real care. Teaching and mentoring matter to me just as much as research. I trained students in competitive programming and robotics at MBSTU, taught machine learning at UGA, and mentored undergraduates through the UGA Mentor Program. I hope to keep building a lab culture where curious students from any background can do work that matters.

    Outside the lab, I solve ACM-style problems, read good literature, and travel whenever I can. I speak Bangla, English, and Bahasa Indonesia.

    I am open to collaboration on spatial genomics, virtual cell models, and medical AI agents. Feel free to email me.
